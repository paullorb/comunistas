# Code of conduct

This project documents state violence against real people, many of whom are alive or have living relatives. Contributors come to it with different histories and different politics. The project works only if discussion stays on the documents.

## What we expect

- Argue from sources. "The ruling says X, on page N" settles things; "everyone knows" does not.
- Assume good faith in a person who brings a source, even when you dislike the conclusion.
- Write about victims, and about people accused or convicted, in plain factual language.
- Accept correction. Being wrong about a date is normal; defending it after the document is on the table is not.

## What is not acceptable

- Denying, celebrating or mocking the killings, disappearances, torture or political imprisonment that the official commissions documented.
- Harassment, insults or personal attacks; slurs of any kind; attacks on someone for their nationality, politics, ethnicity, gender, sexuality, religion, age or disability.
- Publishing someone's private information, or naming private individuals as perpetrators or collaborators without a court ruling or official document.
- Using issues or pull requests for party propaganda or campaigning, from any side.
- Sustained bad-faith argument: repeating a settled point, flooding threads, or misrepresenting what a source says.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove comments, close or lock issues and pull requests, and block accounts. Usually that goes: a note asking you to stop, then a temporary block, then a permanent one. Serious cases skip steps.

To report a problem, use GitHub's "Report content" option on the comment, or contact the maintainer privately through the contact options on their GitHub profile. Reports are kept confidential.

This code applies in the repository and anywhere someone is representing the project. It is adapted in spirit from the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/).
